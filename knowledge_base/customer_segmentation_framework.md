# Customer Segmentation Framework

## Overview
Customer segmentation is the process of dividing a broad consumer or business market into sub-groups based on shared characteristics. This framework outlines various segmentation models, when to use them, and strategies for leveraging segments to drive personalization, higher conversion rates, and better customer retention.

## Core Segmentation Models

### 1. RFM Analysis (Recency, Frequency, Monetary)
A quantitative approach used primarily in e-commerce and retail to identify the best customers based on their purchase history.
- **Recency:** How recently did the customer purchase? (e.g., Days since last order)
- **Frequency:** How often do they purchase? (e.g., Total orders in 12 months)
- **Monetary:** How much do they spend? (e.g., Total customer lifetime spend)
- **Application:** Use RFM to identify "Champions" (High R, F, M) for VIP treatment and "At-Risk" customers (Low R, High F, M) for win-back campaigns.

### 2. Demographic Segmentation
Categorizing customers based on objective socio-economic characteristics.
- **Variables:** Age, gender, income, education, occupation, marital status, company size (B2B), industry (B2B).
- **When to Use:** Top-of-funnel targeting, product-market fit analysis, and broad media buying. 
- **Limitations:** Assumes people with the same demographics have the same needs, which is often false.

### 3. Psychographic Segmentation
Grouping customers based on psychological traits, beliefs, and lifestyles.
- **Variables:** Values, attitudes, interests, lifestyle, personality traits, political affiliations.
- **When to Use:** Crafting messaging, developing brand voice, and creating emotionally resonant ad copy.
- **How to Gather Data:** Surveys, focus groups, social listening, and third-party data providers.

### 4. Behavioral Segmentation
Dividing customers based on their direct interactions with the brand or product.
- **Variables:** Website browsing habits, feature usage, purchase history, email engagement, cart abandonment.
- **When to Use:** Trigger-based marketing (e.g., sending an email when a user abandons a cart), personalization, and retargeting.

### 5. Needs-Based & Value-Based Segmentation
- **Needs-Based:** Segmenting by the specific problem the customer is trying to solve. Essential for B2B and SaaS (e.g., "Needs time-saving automation" vs. "Needs cost reduction").
- **Value-Based:** Segmenting by the economic value of the customer to the business. Focuses marketing efforts on acquiring more customers resembling the highest LTV segment.

## Execution and Strategy

### Segment Sizing and Profiling
- **Sizing:** A segment must be large enough to warrant a dedicated strategy (Substantiality). If a segment only represents 1% of the Total Addressable Market (TAM) but requires heavy resources, it may not be viable.
- **Profiling:** Develop buyer personas for each primary segment. A profile should include the segment's demographics, primary pain points, preferred channels, and buying triggers.

### Avoiding Over-Segmentation
Over-segmentation occurs when segments become too small or too granular, leading to operational inefficiency.
- **Symptoms:** Hundreds of email lists with low open rates, ad campaigns with "audience too narrow" warnings, and content creation bottlenecks.
- **Solution:** Apply the MECE principle (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive). Consolidate micro-segments into broader behavioral cohorts if they respond to the same messaging.

## Decision Criteria and Benchmarks
- **Identifiability:** Can you easily identify and measure the segment? (Demographics are easy; psychographics are hard).
- **Accessibility:** Can you reach the segment via marketing channels?
- **Actionability:** Can your business realistically create products or campaigns for this segment?

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. **Static Segmentation:** Treating segments as permanent. A customer's segment changes (e.g., moving from a "student" to "professional" demographic, or changing behavior).
2. **Siloed Data:** Using different segmentation models across departments (e.g., Sales uses company size, Marketing uses behavioral data). Unify data in a Customer Data Platform (CDP).
3. **Ignoring the "Average" Customer:** Focusing too heavily on the extreme niches and ignoring the core segment that generates the most reliable revenue.

## Actionable Recommendations
- Start with Behavioral and RFM segmentation, as they rely on first-party data you already own and offer immediate ROI.
- Layer on Demographic and Psychographic data later to refine messaging.
- Regularly purge or re-classify inactive segments to maintain database hygiene and reduce marketing automation costs.
