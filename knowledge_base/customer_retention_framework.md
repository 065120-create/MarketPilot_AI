# Customer Retention Framework

## Overview
Customer retention is the strategy of keeping existing customers engaged and profitable over time. Because acquiring a new customer is significantly more expensive than retaining an existing one, a strong retention framework is the engine of sustainable profitability.

## Core Retention Metrics

### Customer Churn Rate
- **Definition:** The percentage of customers who stop doing business with you over a given period.
- **Formula:** `(Customers Lost During Period / Total Customers at Start of Period) × 100`
- **Benchmark:** Good SaaS churn is < 5% annually; B2C subscription churn is typically higher (5-10% monthly).

### Net Promoter Score (NPS)
- **Definition:** Measures customer loyalty based on one question: "How likely are you to recommend us?"
- **Calculation:** `% Promoters (score 9-10) - % Detractors (score 0-6)`

### Customer Satisfaction (CSAT) and Customer Effort Score (CES)
- **CSAT:** Measures satisfaction with a specific interaction (e.g., support ticket resolution).
- **CES:** Measures how easy it was for the customer to get their issue resolved or use a feature. Lower effort correlates strongly with higher retention.

## Identifying Churn Risks

### Churn Prediction Indicators
Relying on historical churn (who already left) is reactive. You must identify predictive indicators of churn (who is going to leave).
- **Usage Drop-off:** A sudden decrease in login frequency or feature usage.
- **Support Ticket Volume:** A spike in complaints or, conversely, total silence after a period of high activity.
- **Payment Failures:** Passive churn accounts for up to 40% of lost revenue. Failed credit card charges must be addressed immediately via dunning sequences.
- **Champion Departure:** In B2B, if your primary contact leaves the client company, the account is immediately at high risk.

## Retention Strategies

### 1. Onboarding Optimization
The seeds of churn are planted during onboarding. If a user doesn't experience the product's core value (the "Aha!" moment) within the first 7-14 days, they are highly likely to churn.
- **Strategy:** Map the shortest path to value. Use guided tours, personalized welcome emails, and gamification to ensure early adoption.

### 2. Engagement Loops and Lifecycle Marketing
- **Milestone Emails:** Celebrate customer wins (e.g., "You've saved 10 hours this week!").
- **Feature Discovery:** Gradually introduce advanced features based on user maturity, preventing them from feeling overwhelmed initially but keeping them engaged long-term.

### 3. Loyalty Programs
- **Points-based or Tier-based:** Incentivize repeat purchases by offering exclusive discounts, early access to products, or VIP support (e.g., Sephora Beauty Insider, airline status).

### 4. Win-Back Campaigns
- **Targeting:** Identify customers who recently lapsed.
- **Offer:** Provide a compelling reason to return (e.g., a high-value discount, an announcement of major product improvements fixing their previous complaints).

## Cohort Analysis
Cohort analysis groups customers based on their acquisition date to track their retention over time.
- **Why it matters:** It shows if your retention is improving or degrading over time. If the Q3 cohort retains better than the Q1 cohort, your product/onboarding improvements are working.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. **Ignoring Passive Churn:** Failing to implement dunning management (automated retries and emails for failed payments).
2. **Treating All Churn Equally:** Losing a highly profitable, 5-year customer is much worse than losing a 1-month trial user. Prioritize retention efforts based on customer LTV.
3. **Survey Fatigue:** Bombarding customers with NPS and CSAT surveys after every minor interaction, leading to low response rates and annoyance.

## Actionable Recommendations
- Conduct Exit Interviews for lost accounts, especially in B2B. Categorize churn reasons (e.g., Pricing, Competitor, Missing Feature) to inform product strategy.
- Build a "Customer Health Score" combining usage data, NPS, and support tickets to automatically flag at-risk accounts for Customer Success intervention.
- Shift budget from Acquisition to Retention if your LTV:CAC ratio drops below 3:1 due to high churn. A leaky bucket cannot be fixed by pouring more water into it.
